STAGE 01

AI demand, IT load & rack density

AI workload changes the power stack through average IT load, rack density and redundancy design. GPU unit shipment is a useful input, but it is not a substitute for measuring MW or GW.

What changes the demand signal
Platform mix · utilization · networking / redundancy
1
AI-server IT loadPRIMARY PHYSICAL DRIVER
The relevant numerator for rack power, server power conversion and direct server heat.
›
Thesis

AI-server IT load grows with total data-center electricity and the AI-server share of that electricity. It is an equipment category, not an AI-facility allocation.

Catalyst

Higher rack density, a larger AI share of facility electricity or accelerated deployment of large AI clusters.

Risk

Confusing installed accelerator nameplate with average power load, or using an AI-facility PUE twice.

Monitor

GW of AI-server IT load; annual MW additions; AI share of facility electricity.

2
Rack density & topologyDESIGN-LEVEL MULTIPLIER
Higher kW/rack raises the required performance of power shelves, busway, connectors, protection and liquid cooling.
›
Thesis

Density raises value per rack only where electrical and thermal design changes; it does not automatically lift every data-center equipment category.

Catalyst

Reference architectures that specify higher-density power shelves, busways or direct-to-chip liquid cooling.

Risk

Using a single vendor's nameplate rack power as a universal market average.

Monitor

kW/rack, voltage architecture, power-shelf count, cooling attachment rate.

STAGE 02

Grid access, procurement & interconnection

Time-to-power is often limited before a data hall is constructed. Interconnection capacity depends on facility load and an explicit utilization assumption; it is neither generation capacity nor IT nameplate.

Constraint composition
Interconnection · transmission · permitting / site
1
Utility interconnectionCHOKEPOINT
The capacity and timing to connect a facility to reliable upstream power.
›
Thesis

Interconnection is a deployability constraint: without it, equipment orders and data-hall construction cannot turn into operating AI load.

Catalyst

Signed utility agreements, completed substation work and disclosed energization dates.

Risk

Assuming the gross interconnection queue is equivalent to executable load, or confusing contracted MW with average load.

Monitor

MW contracted, energization date, curtailment terms, utilization and reliability requirement.

2
Transmission & substation expansionSITE-LEVEL ENABLEMENT
A constraint pool spanning utility capital plans, approvals, land, engineering and equipment lead time.
›
Thesis

Grid expansion becomes valuable when it releases the bottleneck to deployable MW, not merely when sector capex rises.

Catalyst

Permits, approved utility capex and construction milestones that pull an energization date forward.

Risk

Long-duration regulatory processes and local constraints can dominate equipment availability.

Monitor

Approved projects, queue duration, substation MW, transmission completion date.

STAGE 03

HV / MV distribution, transformers & protection

Transformer and switchgear demand is not one homogeneous market. The investable question is whether a product is safety-critical, specification-gated, scarce in lead time and material to the delivery date.

Why this layer matters
Lead time · protection · qualification
1
Large power transformersCHOKEPOINT
High-voltage transformation with multi-year lead-time exposure and project-specific specification.
›
Thesis

A transformer becomes a binding constraint when its lead time exceeds the remaining schedule to energize the facility.

Catalyst

Order slots, factory capacity additions and large-project delivery schedules.

Risk

Generalizing one equipment lead-time range to all voltage classes and geographies.

Monitor

Lead time, delivery slot, voltage rating, testing capacity, order-to-service calendar.

2
MV switchgear & selective protectionSAFETY-CRITICAL
Controls and isolates faults while preserving uptime in increasingly dense electrical systems.
›
Thesis

Its value is linked to protection coordination, fault interruption and uptime, not simply to aggregate MW.

Catalyst

Higher redundancy specifications, utility requirements and proven performance at high-density campuses.

Risk

Standardized equipment pricing can remain competitive even when the overall campus power build is strong.

Monitor

Order backlog, voltage class, certification, uptime tier, service attachment.

STAGE 04

UPS, energy storage & power conversion

UPS and energy storage protect continuity during upstream disturbances. DC architectures can alter conversion losses and equipment mix, but their adoption must be assessed architecture by architecture.

System function
Continuity · conversion · efficiency
1
UPS and backup chainUPTIME-CENTRIC
Bridges interruption windows and coordinates with longer-duration backup power.
›
Thesis

Value is tied to redundancy, quality of power and availability requirements, not just peak facility load.

Catalyst

Higher tiers of availability, larger campus phases and changing battery architecture.

Risk

Backup power sizing depends on redundancy, duration and operating philosophy; it cannot be inferred from average facility load alone.

Monitor

MW rating, redundancy configuration, efficiency, battery chemistry, service contracts.

2
Rectification and DC power architectureEFFICIENCY / DENSITY PATHWAY
Can reduce conversion stages or move power closer to the load, depending on architecture.
›
Thesis

Higher density increases the value of efficient conversion and compact electrical delivery, but technical feasibility is not the same as broad commercial adoption.

Catalyst

Reference architecture adoption, hyperscaler standards and field efficiency data.

Risk

Overstating HVDC or alternative architectures before their installed base and supply chain are visible.

Monitor

Conversion stages, measured efficiency, voltage standard, deployed MW and design wins.

STAGE 05

Rack delivery & power electronics

At the rack, a higher-density system needs lower-loss delivery, appropriate voltage conversion, thermal design and controlled fault isolation. The relevant demand signal is the number of high-density racks and their architecture.

Value-content drivers
Density · conversion · thermal integration
1
Busway, PDU & power shelvesDENSITY BENEFICIARY
Moves electrical power through the white space and into rack-level distribution and conversion.
›
Thesis

Power content per rack can rise with density, provided a given system's busway, PDU and power-shelf configuration actually changes.

Catalyst

Higher rack-power specifications and disclosed deployment of denser AI reference architectures.

Risk

Counting nameplate PSUs as an automatic measure of real power demand or revenue content.

Monitor

kW/rack, power shelves per rack, conversion efficiency, qualification status.

2
Power semiconductors, magnetics & protectionCOMPONENT-LEVEL LEVERAGE
Determines conversion efficiency, thermal burden, transient response and protection at high power density.
›
Thesis

Component leverage depends on content per converted kW, qualification, reliability and design-in position rather than simply being “AI adjacent.”

Catalyst

Higher-voltage designs, improved efficiency requirements and validated high-density platform adoption.

Risk

Alternative device technologies may coexist; technical narratives need evidence of volume design wins.

Monitor

Content per kW, efficiency, thermal rating, qualification cycle and customer concentration.

FEEDER A

Thermal management & cooling

Cooling supports deployable AI load and affects PUE. Direct liquid duty is measured against AI-server IT heat; total facility heat rejection is a separate facility-level measure and must not be added to it.

Thermal scope
Server heat · liquid path · facility heat rejection
1
Direct liquid coolingDENSITY BENEFICIARY
Cold plates, CDUs, pumps and heat exchangers that remove heat from AI-server equipment.
›
Thesis

Direct liquid cooling duty scales with AI-server IT heat and the liquid-cooled share, not with all facility electricity.

Catalyst

Higher-density platforms and increasing liquid-cooling attachment in deployed AI clusters.

Risk

Mixing direct IT cooling duty with total facility heat rejection, leading to double counting.

Monitor

GWth direct duty, attachment rate, CDU capacity, coolant temperature and deployment quality.

2
Facility heat rejectionPUE AND SITE CAPACITY
Moves heat from the facility to the ambient environment through the chosen cooling plant architecture.
›
Thesis

At the facility level, nearly all electrical input eventually becomes heat. The value question is the architecture required to reject it reliably and efficiently.

Catalyst

Campus phases with larger MW, climate-specific design needs and water or power constraints.

Risk

Cooling topology varies by climate, water availability, redundancy and design temperature.

Monitor

Facility MW, PUE, WUE where relevant, cooling-plant type and commissioning date.

Constraint concentration

The four chokepoints

These are system-level constraints. They should be validated separately from any claim about a particular company’s market share, pricing power or earnings exposure.

01 · Grid

Interconnection and deliverable upstream capacity

Capacity must be available, contracted, engineered and energized. A headline grid queue is not a reliable proxy for deployable data-center load.

Evidence to monitorSigned MW, energization date, transmission / substation milestones.
02 · Campus

Large transformer lead time

Long lead items can determine whether a planned 2030 facility is physically able to receive power on schedule.

Evidence to monitorFactory slot, voltage class, testing capacity and delivery date.
03 · Rack

High-density power delivery qualification

Higher power requires coordination across busway, conversion, protection, connectors and thermal management—not a single-component upgrade.

Evidence to monitorReference architecture, validated rack power and field reliability.
04 · Thermal

Liquid-path and heat-rejection integration

Cooling becomes a deployment constraint when it limits supported rack density, PUE, site design or commissioning speed.

Evidence to monitorLiquid-cooling attachment, CDU capacity, cooling-plant commissioning.

Economics

Where value may migrate

Demand growth alone is not a margin thesis. The difference is whether an activity owns a safety, qualification, lead-time or system-integration constraint.

Constraint owners / margin defenders

  • Structure · specification- or qualification-gated
  • Economics · safety-critical, high switching cost or scarce delivery slot
  • Evidence · backlog quality, lead time, price realization, service attach
  • Watchlist · transformers, protection, validated high-density subsystems
VALUE SHIFTS
WITH CONSTRAINT

Capacity followers / execution-sensitive

  • Structure · scalable equipment, engineering or commodity exposure
  • Economics · strong demand, but supply may arrive quickly
  • Evidence · book-to-bill, utilization, gross margin and project delivery
  • Watchlist · standardized systems and broad construction exposure
Method and boundaries

What this atlas measures

  1. Facility electricity is the annual electricity used by data-center facilities.
  2. Average facility load is facility electricity converted from TWh to GW.
  3. AI-server electricity is an IT-equipment category divided by total facility electricity; no extra AI PUE is applied.
  4. Direct liquid duty applies only to AI-server IT heat. Total facility heat rejection is shown separately.
  5. Interconnection capacity is average facility load divided by an explicit utilization input.

Boundary: the macro model does not infer equipment revenue, company market share or company valuation. Those claims require separate bill-of-materials, customer and financial evidence.
